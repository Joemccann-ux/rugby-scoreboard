Scoreboard look

Scoreboard connection

On "Automatic", the app uses the first screen that isn't your laptop and takes it over full screen as soon as it's plugged in. It only does this while the app is open – close the app and your screens go back to normal.

Handy if you're doing other things on the laptop. While it's on, the number pad won't type numbers in other programs.

Getting the picture onto the scoreboard

  1. On the Mac: System Settings → Displays. Make sure the scoreboard output is extended (not mirrored), and make your MacBook the main display.On Windows: press Windows key + P and choose Extend. Then in Settings → System → Display, click your laptop screen and tick Make this my main display.
  2. Click Open scoreboard window (top right), then drag that window onto the scoreboard screen.
  3. Double-click inside it to make it full screen. (Press Esc in that window to leave full screen.)
  4. Turn on Test pattern below. You should see a red border right round the edge of the LED screen. If it's cut off or shifted, adjust the position and size below.

Keep computer awake: checking…  ·  A red warning appears at the top if the scoreboard window ends up back on your laptop screen (e.g. the HDMI cable comes out).

Position & size (in screen pixels)

Default is 400 × 270 in the top-left corner, which matches a pixel-mapped LED controller (it only shows that corner of the computer's picture).

Monitor copy

The LED screen only shows the top-left corner, so the rest of the picture is free. If you feed your monitor from an HDMI splitter, this gives you a big, easy-to-read view of exactly what's on the board. Turn it off if anything stray appears on the LED.
